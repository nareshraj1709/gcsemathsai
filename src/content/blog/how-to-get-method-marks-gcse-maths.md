Every year, students leave hundreds of marks on the table — not because they don't know the maths, but because they rub out their working when they get an answer they're not sure about. In GCSE maths, **method marks** can mean the difference between sitting on a grade boundary and clearing it. Understanding how they work — and how to guarantee them — is one of the highest-value exam skills you can build. AQA (8300), Edexcel (1MA1), and OCR (J560) all use method marks on longer multi-step questions, typically appearing from question 8 onwards on Paper 2 and Paper 3.

## What You Need to Know First

Before we go into how to earn method marks, make sure you understand these basics:

- **M marks (method marks):** awarded for demonstrating a correct method, even if you make an arithmetic slip
- **A marks (accuracy marks):** awarded for getting the final answer correct — these usually depend on the relevant M mark being earned first
- **B marks (independent marks):** awarded for a correct standalone value or statement; these don't depend on method
- **"Error carried forward" (ECF):** if you carry a wrong answer from an earlier part into a later part and use it correctly, you can still earn the method mark for the later part
- **Mark schemes list multiple valid methods:** there is rarely one correct approach — examiners award marks for any complete, correct method shown on the page

Understanding this structure means you approach multi-step questions differently. The goal isn't just to get the right answer — it's to show every step of your reasoning.

## How Method Marks Are Awarded

The mark scheme for any GCSE maths question breaks down marks into components. For a 4-mark question, a typical breakdown looks like this:

- **M1** — setting up the correct equation or writing the right formula
- **M1** — a correct algebraic or arithmetic step that follows from the setup
- **A1** — reaching the correct value
- **A1** — correct units, rounding, or interpretation stated

The critical point: **you can earn both M marks even with a wrong final answer**, as long as your working shows you used the right method.

**Worked Example 1 — Area of a Composite Shape**

*Question:* A shape is made of a rectangle and a semicircle. The rectangle is 10 cm wide and 6 cm tall. The semicircle sits on top of the rectangle with a diameter of 10 cm. Find the total area. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [3 marks]

*Mark scheme:*
- M1 — correct method for area of rectangle: 10 × 6
- M1 — correct method for area of semicircle: ½ × π × 5²
- A1 — correct total: 99.3 cm²

*Student working (with an arithmetic slip on the semicircle):*

Area of rectangle = 10 × 6 = 60 cm²

Area of semicircle = ½ × π × r² = ½ × π × 5² = ½ × π × 25 = **37.5 cm²** *(error — should be 39.3 cm²)*

Total area = 60 + 37.5 = **97.5 cm²** *(wrong final answer)*

*Marks awarded:* M1 M1 A0 — **2 out of 3**

The student still earns both method marks because the working clearly shows the correct formula being applied to the correct values. The A mark is lost for the wrong answer, but 2 marks are secured from correct method. Without that working visible, a wrong final answer earns zero.

The rule is simple: always write the formula before substituting numbers. Examiners award the M1 at the formula stage, not the answer stage.

## Earning Marks Through Error Carried Forward

The **error carried forward (ECF)** rule lets you earn marks in later parts of a question even when an earlier part went wrong — provided you use your wrong answer correctly in the method.

**Worked Example 2 — Multi-Step Percentage Problem**

*Question:*

(a) A jacket costs £80. Find 35% of £80. [1 mark]

(b) The jacket is reduced by 35%. Find the sale price. [1 mark]

(c) A loyalty card gives Laura a further 10% off the sale price. How much does she pay in total? [2 marks]

*Student working:*

(a) 35% of £80 = 0.35 × 80 = **£29** *(wrong — correct answer is £28)*

(b) Sale price = 80 − 29 = **£51** *(wrong, but follows correctly from their part (a))*

(c) 10% of £51 = £5.10

Laura pays: 51 − 5.10 = **£45.90**

*Marks awarded:*
- (a) **A0** — wrong answer (£28 is correct)
- (b) **M1 A1(ft)** — correct subtraction method; ECF applies because they used their part (a) answer correctly
- (c) **M1 A1(ft)** — correct 10% method and subtraction; ECF applies again

The student lost just 1 mark from the arithmetic slip in (a), but still earned 4 marks across (b) and (c). That's a strong recovery — entirely because the working was visible at every step. Had the student written only the final answer for each part, the marker would have no basis for awarding ECF.

This is why abandoning a question halfway through because part (a) went wrong is one of the most expensive mistakes in GCSE maths. Keep going. Use your answer and work through the rest.

## Common Mistakes Students Make

- **Rubbing out working when the answer looks wrong.** This is the single costliest error. Leave every line of working on the page — it may still earn M marks even if the final answer is wrong.
- **Writing only the final answer on multi-step questions.** A bare answer earns zero marks if it's wrong. The M marks only exist in the working.
- **Not stating the formula before substituting.** Writing "= ½ × π × 25" without first writing "area of semicircle = ½πr²" makes it harder for the examiner to award the M1 at the formula stage.
- **Collapsing two method steps into one.** If you do too much mentally, the examiner cannot see the individual M marks being earned. Write each step as its own line.
- **Stopping after a wrong answer in part (a).** Students assume that if part (a) is wrong, parts (b) and (c) are lost. They're not — ECF rules mean correct method on your own wrong figures still earns marks.

## Exam Tips for Method Marks

- **Write every step, even obvious ones.** "I simplified 60/100 to 3/5" is worth writing. Examiners can only award marks for what is on the page.
- **State the formula first for every standard topic.** Pythagoras: c² = a² + b². Trigonometry: sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse. Volume of a prism: V = area of cross-section × length. Write it, then substitute — the M1 is almost always at the formula stage.
- **Cross out, don't erase.** A single neat line through a wrong attempt leaves it readable. Examiners can still award an M mark from crossed-out working if it shows the right method.
- **AQA's mark schemes** often specify "M1 for a complete method" — meaning every step must be shown. Edexcel and OCR work similarly. For longer questions, treat each mark as a separate step that needs its own line.
- **If you think you've gone wrong, write a brief note.** Something like "I think I made an error in step 2" tells the examiner to look closely at your earlier working for method marks. It takes five seconds and can recover a mark.
