Cumulative frequency is one of those topics that looks complicated on paper but follows a very clear, repeatable method. It often links to box plots, so mastering both together is time well spent.

## Drawing a Cumulative Frequency Graph

**Cumulative frequency** means the running total of frequencies as you move through the data. You always plot cumulative frequency against the **upper class boundary** of each group — never the midpoint.

**Step 1:** Add a cumulative frequency column to your table by adding up frequencies as you go.

**Step 2:** Write down the coordinates. Each point is (upper class boundary, cumulative frequency).

**Step 3:** Plot the points and join them with a smooth curve. The curve always starts at (lowest value, 0) — it must go through the origin of your data, not the graph's origin.

**Step 4:** Label your axes. The y-axis is cumulative frequency; the x-axis is the variable being measured.

### Worked Example 1

The table below shows the times (in minutes) 80 students took to complete a test.

| Time (minutes) | Frequency |
|----------------|-----------|
| 0 < t ≤ 20     | 5         |
| 20 < t ≤ 40    | 18        |
| 40 < t ≤ 60    | 32        |
| 60 < t ≤ 80    | 20        |
| 80 < t ≤ 100   | 5         |

**Step 1 — Build the cumulative frequency column:**

| Time (minutes) | Frequency | Cumulative Frequency |
|----------------|-----------|----------------------|
| 0 < t ≤ 20     | 5         | 5                    |
| 20 < t ≤ 40    | 18        | 23                   |
| 40 < t ≤ 60    | 32        | 55                   |
| 60 < t ≤ 80    | 20        | 75                   |
| 80 < t ≤ 100   | 5         | 80                   |

**Step 2 — Write the coordinates using upper class boundaries:**

(20, 5), (40, 23), (60, 55), (80, 75), (100, 80)

Also plot the starting point: (0, 0) — because 0 students had finished in 0 minutes.

**Step 3 — Plot and join with a smooth S-shaped curve.** The curve should pass through all six points.

## Finding the Median and Quartiles

Once you have your cumulative frequency graph, you can read off the **median**, **lower quartile** and **upper quartile** using horizontal and vertical lines.

For a dataset of n values:
- **Median** = value at n ÷ 2
- **Lower Quartile (LQ)** = value at n ÷ 4
- **Upper Quartile (UQ)** = value at n ÷ 4 × 3 (or 3n ÷ 4)
- **IQR** = UQ − LQ

### Worked Example 2

Using the graph from the example above (n = 80 students):

- **Median:** Read across from 80 ÷ 2 = **40** on the cumulative frequency axis → draw a horizontal line to the curve → drop down to the x-axis → **Median ≈ 53 minutes**

- **Lower Quartile:** Read across from 80 ÷ 4 = **20** → → **LQ ≈ 38 minutes**

- **Upper Quartile:** Read across from 3 × 80 ÷ 4 = **60** → → **UQ ≈ 66 minutes**

- **IQR** = 66 − 38 = **28 minutes**

These five values — minimum (0), LQ (38), median (53), UQ (66), maximum (100) — are exactly what you need to draw a **box plot**.

To draw the box plot:
1. Draw a number line covering the full range
2. Draw a box from LQ to UQ
3. Draw a vertical line inside the box at the median
4. Draw whiskers out to the minimum and maximum values

## Common Mistakes Students Make

- **Plotting against the midpoint instead of the upper class boundary.** The point (40, 23) uses the upper boundary of 20 < t ≤ 40, which is 40 — not the midpoint 30. This is one of the most common errors.
- **Forgetting to plot the starting point (0, 0).** Without this, your curve won't start in the right place and the shape will be wrong.
- **Joining points with straight lines instead of a smooth curve.** Cumulative frequency graphs should always be a smooth S-shaped curve. Straight lines between points lose the presentation mark.
- **Reading the wrong value from the axis.** When finding the median, go across from the cumulative frequency axis first, then down to the x-axis — not the other way around. Students who go up from the x-axis are guessing.
- **Using n instead of n ÷ 2 for the median.** The median is at the n/2 position, not at n. For 80 values, go across from 40, not 80.

## Exam Tips for Cumulative Frequency

- **Always label your axes** — you'll lose a mark if they're missing. The y-axis must say "Cumulative frequency" and the x-axis must show the variable and units.
- **Use a ruler for the horizontal and vertical construction lines** when reading off the median and quartiles. Examiners expect to see these lines drawn on the graph — they are your working, and some mark schemes award a method mark specifically for them.
- **The final cumulative frequency must equal the total frequency.** Check your last value matches n before you start plotting — it's a quick error-check that takes two seconds.
- **AQA and Edexcel both award method marks for a correct cumulative frequency table** even if the graph is slightly inaccurate. Fill the table in first, carefully, before touching the graph.
- **For box plots, read values off your curve to 1 decimal place if the graph allows it.** Being precise shows the examiner you used the graph properly and earns full marks where another student might lose an accuracy mark.
- **If the question says "use your graph," you must read the answer from the graph** — not calculate it from the table. Examiners check that construction lines are drawn.
