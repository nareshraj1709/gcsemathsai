Paper 1 on all three GCSE Maths specifications — AQA 8300 Paper 1, Edexcel 1MA1 Paper 1, and OCR J560 Component 1 — is sat without a calculator. For many students, this is the paper they dread most. But the non-calculator paper tests a specific set of skills, and once you know the tricks examiners are looking for, you can work faster, check answers more confidently, and stop losing marks to arithmetic errors. The students who score highest on Paper 1 are not necessarily better at maths — they have better mental strategies.

## What You Need to Know First

- The non-calculator paper typically carries 80 marks over 1 hour 30 minutes — that is just over 1 minute per mark
- Questions test your ability to do exact arithmetic, not approximate answers (unlike estimation questions, which are clearly flagged)
- Key topics that appear heavily on Paper 1 include: fractions, surds, standard form, exact trigonometric values, and algebraic manipulation
- The mark scheme gives full credit for correct working even if the final answer is wrong — so always show your method
- The examiner expects neat, organised arithmetic — scatter-gun working is harder to mark and harder to check

## Core Mental Arithmetic Techniques

Reliable mental arithmetic comes from a small set of techniques you can apply to almost any non-calculator calculation. Practise these until they are automatic.

### Multiplying by breaking numbers apart

The key principle: split one factor into parts that are easier to multiply, then add the results. This is called the **distributive property** and it works for any multiplication.

**Worked Example 1**

Calculate 47 × 8.

Method: Split 47 into 40 + 7

40 × 8 = 320

7 × 8 = 56

320 + 56 = **376**

This is far quicker than long multiplication written out in full, and it is less likely to produce an error.

**Worked Example 2**

Calculate 36 × 25.

Method: Notice that 25 = 100 ÷ 4

36 × 25 = 36 × 100 ÷ 4 = 3600 ÷ 4 = **900**

Recognising these "nice" relationships — such as multiplying by 25 being the same as dividing by 4 then multiplying by 100 — saves significant time across the paper.

### Working with fractions without a calculator

Fractions appear on every non-calculator paper. The two most common operations are adding fractions with different denominators and multiplying or dividing mixed numbers.

**Adding fractions:** Find the lowest common denominator, convert both fractions, then add the numerators.

Example: ²⁄₃ + ³⁄₄

LCM of 3 and 4 is 12

²⁄₃ = ⁸⁄₁₂ and ³⁄₄ = ⁹⁄₁₂

⁸⁄₁₂ + ⁹⁄₁₂ = **¹⁷⁄₁₂** = 1⁵⁄₁₂

**Dividing fractions:** Convert any mixed numbers to improper fractions, then flip the second fraction and multiply.

Example: 2½ ÷ ³⁄₄

Convert: 2½ = ⁵⁄₂

Flip and multiply: ⁵⁄₂ × ⁴⁄₃ = ²⁰⁄₆ = ¹⁰⁄₃ = **3¹⁄₃**

### Exact values for trigonometry

On the non-calculator paper, questions often require exact trig values. Memorise this table — it comes up on all three boards:

| Angle | sin | cos | tan |
|-------|-----|-----|-----|
| 0°    | 0   | 1   | 0   |
| 30°   | ½   | √3/2 | 1/√3 |
| 45°   | √2/2 | √2/2 | 1  |
| 60°   | √3/2 | ½  | √3 |
| 90°   | 1   | 0   | undefined |

A question might say: "Given that sin 30° = ½, find the exact value of sin²30° + cos²30°." The answer should come out to 1 (the Pythagorean identity) — knowing exact values lets you verify this without a calculator.

## Estimation and Checking Strategies

The non-calculator paper rewards students who check their work efficiently. The key tool is estimation: replacing exact values with round numbers to verify your answer is in the right ballpark.

### The estimation rule

Round every number to 1 significant figure before performing the calculation. This gives a rough answer in seconds.

**Example:** Estimate 48.3 × 19.7

Round: 50 × 20 = 1000

If your exact calculation gave 952.01, that is close to 1000, so it is plausible. If your calculation gave 95.201, the estimation tells you something has gone wrong — a factor of 10 error, perhaps a decimal in the wrong place.

### Percentage calculations without a calculator

For any percentage calculation, build it from easy multiples:

- 10% = divide by 10
- 5% = halve the 10% value
- 1% = divide by 100
- 25% = divide by 4
- 15% = add 10% and 5%

**Worked Example 3**

Find 17.5% of £240.

10% of £240 = £24

5% of £240 = £12

2.5% of £240 = £6

17.5% = 10% + 5% + 2.5% = £24 + £12 + £6 = **£42**

This method is faster than any written algorithm and produces the exact answer. AQA Paper 1 regularly tests VAT and percentage change using values like 17.5% or 22.5% — practise building these from components.

### Checking with inverse operations

After any calculation, apply the inverse operation quickly to check. If you calculated 5,460 ÷ 12 = 455, verify with 455 × 12:

400 × 12 = 4,800

55 × 12 = 660

4,800 + 660 = 5,460 ✓

Spending 20 seconds checking each answer this way catches errors before you move on — and is much faster than redoing the whole calculation.

## Common Mistakes Students Make

- **Writing untidy arithmetic.** Messy column addition leads to carrying errors. Line up digits carefully — the column method only works when each column is clearly defined.
- **Treating estimation as an exact answer.** The estimation check tells you the order of magnitude. It does not give you the correct decimal places. Always complete the full, exact calculation before writing the answer.
- **Confusing percentage increase and percentage of.** "Increase £80 by 20%" gives £96 (not £16, which is just 20% of the original). Always check whether you need the increase or the new total.
- **Forgetting to simplify fractions.** Many mark schemes require answers in lowest terms. ⁶⁄₉ as an answer will lose the final mark if the mark scheme specifies ²⁄₃. Always check for common factors.
- **Writing mixed number fractions incorrectly.** Students often write 1 and 5/12 as 1.5/12 on the page, which looks like a fraction divided by twelve. Write it clearly: 1⁵⁄₁₂ with the fractional part visibly raised.
- **Not knowing the exact trig values.** These are not given in the formulae booklet on most papers. Leaving a trig answer as "sin 30°" rather than "½" loses the mark on every board.

## Exam Tips for the Non-Calculator Paper

- **Work left to right across the paper, not hardest to easiest.** Skipping around wastes time. Answer every question in order; mark anything you find difficult with a clear asterisk and return to it at the end.
- **Show all your working even for mental methods.** Writing "40 × 8 = 320, 7 × 8 = 56, 320 + 56 = 376" takes five seconds and earns you method marks if the final line is wrong.
- **Use the formulae booklet efficiently.** AQA 8300 provides a formulae sheet. OCR and Edexcel provide one at higher tier. Do not waste time re-deriving things that are given to you — but do familiarise yourself with where each formula appears before the exam.
- **AQA awards marks for correct method in numerical questions.** If your arithmetic goes wrong mid-way through a multi-step non-calculator question, the method mark for setting up the calculation correctly is still available. Write the method first, then compute.
- **Practise without any calculator from now on.** Do not use a calculator even for homework calculations where it is allowed. The more you do by hand, the faster and more accurate you become — there is no shortcut to this.
